## What is measured

The original rigid recordings come from complete 20-second MuJoCo executions using the same scene and downward/sideways force pulses. The controller follows scripted arm poses. The feedback variant increases gripper force from ideal simulated pad shear/normal force ratios; the fixed-grip variant does not. Neither is a trained UniGeom-X model. Contact maps visualize solver contact locations and loads with a display kernel; they are not calibrated pressure images or optical tactile measurements. Relative object slip is an evaluation measurement, not an input to the grip controller.

Saved physics states and contact readouts are sampled every 20 ms; physics advances every 2 ms. The browser interpolates body poses between samples. It holds contact observations at their recorded samples and preserves each run's final success or failure. Physics stepping and the same controller run locally in Live physics. Camera movement does not change the controller's known target.

The original block is rigid, freely moving and held by friction; no weld attaches it to the gripper. The stripe, goal marking and contact-map smoothing are visual aids. The original block does not deform. Neither task claims a damage model or learned vision inference.

## Contact workcell v2

The new rubber mat, laminate tabletop, metal legs and concrete floor use locally
created procedural materials. The original CarryQ wood/denim maps are no longer
loaded by this scene.

The optical tactile illustration follows the physical imaging principle described
by [Yuan, Dong and Adelson, GelSight (2017)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5751610/):
colored directional illumination reveals gel surface slope, and markers reveal
lateral deformation. Visual reference: the real sensor panels in the official
[ManiSkill-ViTac 2025 peg-insertion comparison](https://ai-workshops.github.io/maniskill-vitac-challenge-2025/img/peg-insertion-sim2real.gif).
[ManiFeel](https://zhengtongxu.github.io/manifeel-website/) provides additional
TacRGB / force-field task references.

`tactile.js` is an original, analytic approximation driven by contact normal load,
contact centroid and signed pad-local shear. It assumes a compliant rectangular
imprint, not a calibrated gel mechanics model. The images are neither paper
figures nor sensor-camera measurements, and are not fed back into the controller.
The controller reads ideal MuJoCo contact forces. No external reference image or
video is redistributed as our experimental output.

## Material and table revision

The tabletop area is now exactly twice the first compact table: both horizontal
extents are multiplied by sqrt(2). The rubber mat is 1.06 × 0.74 m. Legs and apron
are repositioned to support the larger top. `materials.js` generates independent
color, micro-height and roughness maps for rubber, laminate, coated metal, brushed
metal and flooring. A local studio environment supplies broad reflections, while
rounded visual edges, foot pads and tray fasteners add construction details.
These details are original procedural geometry and materials; no image assets are
required. Visual bevels preserve the collision boxes' outer dimensions.

## Soft sponge task

The second object is an original reduced elastic proxy: eight compliant box contact
nodes on a free carrier, using native MuJoCo spring joints and frictional
contacts. Surface vertices follow the contact bodies. It is not a volumetric FEM
model or a calibrated material asset. No robot–object attachment is used.
The porous surface texture is generated by our own deterministic procedural
material; no third-party sponge photograph or watermark-bearing texture is used.

Soft records use 1 ms physical integration and 20 ms recording. Compression is
an ideal geometric observation available to the demo controller. The fixed firm
grip and feedback controller both place the object but produce different peak
compression. A 35% demonstration bound does not imply physical damage. The
soft gel illustration spreads the measured contact-node loads; it remains an
analytic visual approximation, not sensor-camera footage.

## Synchronized comparison revision

The two controllers are shown concurrently from a shared clock. Their cameras
are linked, and each has its own tactile and force readouts. The original rigid
recordings are superseded by corrected executions that stop external load pulses
after grip loss. The sponge records use box-shaped compliant contact nodes whose
outer corners match the displayed surface, with adjacent finger/hand collision
geometry enabled. The contact bound remains a demonstration criterion.
